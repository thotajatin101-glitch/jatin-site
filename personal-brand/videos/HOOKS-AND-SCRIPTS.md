# What works in his reels: hooks and scripts (2026-11-21)

**His ask:** check his reference reels, then run two agents, one on hooks and one on scripts, that cross-check each
other and land on what actually works.

**His references:**
- **elliotisacoolguy**: "is TouchDesigner as hard as it looks?"
- **digital_spaghetti**: a podcast clip with Elliot
- **maybe.quoteme**: "i am officially unmaxxing my life"

**Caveat:** nobody watched the clips. Instagram needs a login and the YouTube transcript is locked, so the read on
these three comes from captions, titles, audio credits and outside sources.

**Process:** Agent A researched hooks and Agent B researched scripts and retention. Then each critiqued the other.

## The tone, in one line
**Sincere, said straight. The edit makes the joke.**
- **Elliot:** grand narration over a small struggle. Lowercase, first-person titles that admit a limit ("i suck at
  logo design… so i made 1000 in a day").
- **maybe.quoteme:** a sincere personal take, with the laughs in the sound cues, each one landing right after a line.

Both open with a **confession, not a credential**.

## Settled by the cross-check

| Question | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Open on silence, or on a sound? | **A sound on frame 1, then quiet room tone under his line** (B conceded) | Instagram counts people who skip in the first 3s; dead air at frame 0 reads as a broken upload |
| How many sound cues? | **About 3 joke cues in the whole reel**, each one a recognisable sound doing a joke | B's table drifted to 7 events. Little clicks dilute the jokes; a punch on every cut is try-hard |
| Where does a cue go? | **In the gap after the line**, never on top of it (A conceded) | It reads like a laugh after the line, not over it |
| The best hook? | **"i'm not a developer. i built my website by giving it editing notes."** (both ranked it #1) | It sets up the whole reel, the visual proves it instantly, and it's an angle only he has |
| Open on the Safari dots? | **No. Use them mid-reel** | They're the best punchline; opening on them spends the payoff and turns the reel into a bug story |
| A take line at the end? | **His own words on camera, not a written quote** | "Making something yours is deciding what it isn't" is written to be quoted, which is the try-hard tell |
| How should the turn connect? | **Each beat causes the next ("but" / "so"), never "and then"** | Parker and Stone's rule. The turn has to grow out of the struggle, not just arrive |

## Hook rules
1. Name the problem or the angle in the first 1.5s. The visual proves it by 3s.
2. One sentence, first person, said flat. Lowercase as on-screen text, so it works muted.
3. A confession or a limit, not a credential.
4. Promise only what the reel delivers.
5. Never "you need to", "stop scrolling", "nobody talks about", or "WITHOUT CODING 🤯".
6. If people skip early, re-cut only the first 3 seconds.

## Script rules
1. Write the hook word for word. Everything else is 4–5 *specific* bullets (vague bullets leave you blank on camera).
2. Link the beats with "but" and "so".
3. Say it straight; the cut gets the laugh. No mugging to the lens.
4. Show every failure as a screen recording or a phone photo. Don't just describe it.
5. The take is one soft sentence, found on camera. Never "you should".
6. The caption tells a second joke the video didn't, in lowercase.
7. No outro, no "follow for part 2". End on the site working, matching the opening frame so it loops.

## What's actually proven vs. opinion
- **Proven** (Instagram's own head, Mosseri):
  - the first 3s decide the skip;
  - on-screen text helps;
  - original audio helps reach.
- **Creator practice, not proof:**
  - sound-effect timing;
  - talking points over a full script;
  - loops boosting replays.
- **Unverified:** the "1.4 second" attention figure that gets passed around.
