# How organic talking-head reels are written (research, 2026-11-21)

Done before round 4 of the reel, at his ask: understand "how the most organic and creative scripts are made in this
particular type of content and … what works and what does'nt".

## What's well sourced
- **Instagram ranks reels on watch time, likes per reach and sends per reach.** Sends weigh most for reaching people
  who don't follow you ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-shares-algorithm-insights-2025/738034/)).
  So a reel earns its reach by holding interest and by saying something a person wants to pass on, not by asking.
- **Ira Glass's "taste gap" clip was unplanned.** He hadn't prepared for that shoot, and it spread years later as a fan
  edit. It worked because it named a feeling he'd really had in his 20s
  ([BuzzFeed](https://www.buzzfeed.com/adambvary/ira-glass-on-watching-himself-go-viral),
  [Kottke](https://also.kottke.org/11/04/your-taste-is-why-your-own-work-disappoints-you)).
- **Audiences are tired of polish and of the "explainer" voice.** Digiday (Jan 2026) quotes an agency CMO: "we crave
  imperfection" ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/after-an-oversaturation-of-ai-generated-content-creators-authenticity-and-messiness-are-in-high-demand/)).
  Dazed (Sept 2026) criticises bold-claim openers and performed smartness
  ([Dazed](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/70929/1/thoughtfluencers-hot-takes-tiny-mics-explainer-epidemic-instagram-tiktok)).

## What's common practice (creator advice, not proof)
- **Signs a line was written, not spoken:**
  - three-part escalating lines
  - repeated "not X, but Y"
  - every sentence the same length
  - announcing the structure
  - a recap at the end
- **What real speech has instead:** contractions, one idea per sentence, the same word repeated rather than a synonym,
  and odd exact numbers.
- **Natural hooks:**
  - open in the middle of a moment, or on one specific detail
  - a confession or a question you really have works too
  - pay the hook off early, because vague teasing reads as clickbait
- **Keeping people watching:**
  - show each thing on screen the moment it's named
  - one small turn ("then I realised…")
  - cut B-roll to the beats of the speech
- **What reads as try-hard:**
  - "here's what nobody tells you", "stop doing X", fake contrarian takes
  - false hooks
  - numbered-list voice
  - one-word punch lines, slogans as the lesson
  - "comment X for…" captions
- **Endings:** end on what you actually do or a question you're still asking. No recap, no call to action.
- **Delivery:**
  - write the first and last lines word for word, bullet the middle
  - do one take with no notes first
  - over-rehearsing sounds recited

## Rules we use for his reels
1. Open on a true, specific moment. No claim he'd have to walk back.
2. Name real things (Persona 5 Royal, a cut he made), never "stuff I love".
3. Don't announce the lesson; let it come out of the story.
4. Contractions, one idea per sentence, uneven sentence lengths.
5. At most one "not X but Y". No three-part escalations, no one-word punch lines, no slogans.
6. Admit one thing he's unsure of.
7. Prove the turn on screen.
8. End on what he does now. No recap, no CTA.
9. Caption: one or two plain lines of context, no engagement bait.
