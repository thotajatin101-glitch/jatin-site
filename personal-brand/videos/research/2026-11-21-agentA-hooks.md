## 2. Observed from real creators
- **Elliot's formula is first-person, lowercase, and admits the limit up front:** "i suck at X… so I [absurd effort]", "i [did X] for the first time". The humble premise is the joke and the effort is the stakes. Nothing in it is a superlative or a "you". The question title ("is X as hard as it looks?") puts the viewer's own doubt into words.
- **His deadpan comes from contrast.** Grand narration over something small ("this is his story... just for you") sends up the epic format while he still does the real work.
- **maybe.quoteme's essay reels are a sincere point of view.** The jokes are the sound cues, not the words: a record scratch for a turn, crickets for awkwardness, a punch for impact, Eye of the Tiger for mock-heroics. Each cue lands on one line, the way a laugh track would. Her captions undercut the piece ("lol", "??"), so the earnest essay never reads as preachy.
- **Shared by both:** a status confession instead of a credential ("corporate job, creative brain", "sick man learning TouchDesigner"). Neither tells you why to watch. They tell you what went wrong.

## 3. Opinion: what works in this niche and what reads try-hard
**Works:** say the result or the problem in the first breath; a specific, real failure (dots in Safari, lag on a friend's laptop); a visual that proves the line before he finishes it; one sound cue that does the joke so his face can stay flat.
**Try-hard / clickbait:** "STOP scrolling", "nobody talks about this", "I built a website WITHOUT CODING 🤯", "here's how YOU can…", leaning into the lens with big energy, a trending song under the talk, and naming the references (which he doesn't want anyway).

**Sound as part of the hook (opinion, from the observed patterns):**
- The first frame needs a sound event: a mouse click, the site's own impact hit, or a record scratch on the cut. It should not open on a music bed fading in.
- Use silence or room tone under the deadpan line, then let the cue fire on the reveal.
- Use at most one comic cue in the first 3s. Treat cues as punctuation, roughly one per beat.
- Musical irony works when the image backs it up: epic music under a dot-grid Safari bug. A music swell under a big claim with nothing behind it doesn't.

## 4. Hook rules
1. Say the problem or the result in the first 1.5s, before any intro or "so".
2. Lowercase, first person, one sentence, written like a text to a friend.
3. Lead with a real failure, not a credential. "i'm not a developer" beats "I built a site".
4. The visual has to prove the line inside 3s: the site slamming in, the Safari dots, a laggy screen recording.
5. Keep the face deadpan and let the sound be funny. Use one cue, timed to a cut.
6. Text on screen says the hook in short form, so it works with the sound off.
7. Promise only what the reel shows. The payoff must match.
8. Never say "you need to", "stop scrolling", or "nobody talks about".
9. Don't name the references. Call them "a video game menu", or show the result without explaining.
10. Test it: if the skip rate is high, re-cut only the first 3s.

## 5. Candidate hooks (spoken / on-screen text / visual / sound)
1. **"i'm not a developer. i built my website by giving it editing notes."**
   Text: *built a website with editing notes*. Visual: a hard cut from his face to the site's intro slamming in. Sound: silence, then the site's own impact hit.
2. **"my friend opened my portfolio in safari and saw… dots."**
   Text: *my portfolio, in safari:*. Visual: a screen of grey dots where photos should be. Sound: crickets.
3. **"the website looked amazing. on my laptop."**
   Text: *it worked on my laptop*. Visual: smooth on his screen, then a judder cut to a friend's laptop. Sound: a record scratch on the cut.
4. **"is building a website as hard as it looks? (for a guy who edits videos)"**
   Text: *an editor builds a website*. Visual: his DaVinci timeline next to the website. Sound: a mouse click on the cut.
5. **"the best edit i made to my website was deleting stuff."**
   Text: *cutting until it was mine*. Visual: pieces vanishing from the menu, one per beat. Sound: a punch hit on each cut.

My pick is #1. It sets up the whole reel (an editor's notes turned into a site), the visual pays it off at once, and #2 and #3 can follow as the mid-reel beats.

## Critique of B

**Where B contradicts my hooks, and who's right**
- **B's opening line breaks B's own rule 7.** The 0–3s line, "so I built my own website. I'm not a developer.", opens on "so", which rule 7 says to cut. It also makes a generic claim that thousands of reels make. My rule 1 holds: the specific angle (editing notes) or the specific failure has to come in the first breath. Right now the specific angle doesn't arrive until 3–9s, which is after the Skip Rate window.
- **B is right that the face should stay flat while the site plays.** But "I built a website" over a pretty site promises a showcase. The reel is actually about failing and then cutting. That makes it a mild hook–payoff mismatch.

**Weak or try-hard in disguise**
- The comic-timing and SFX rules rest on vendor blogs (air.io, tokcount, fish.audio). B labels the rewatch claim as anecdotal, which is fair.
- The "podcast-clip look" doesn't apply. He is solo on camera.
- The take, "making something yours is mostly deciding what it isn't", is an aphorism written to be quoted. That is a lesson-as-rule wearing a "maybe", which is the exact try-hard tell B lists. B flags it as a placeholder; it should go.

**Does the structure keep my rules?**
- Promise vs delivery: it mostly works, and the loop ending pays off well.
- Problem within 1.5s: no. Nothing goes wrong until 9s.
- The turn at 21s says "therefore", but nothing causes it. Cutting things has to come from the lag, e.g. "so I started cutting things to make it run".

**Sound density**
- B's rule says 3–4 cues. The table has a click per note (about 3), a scratch, crickets, music returning and a resolve, so roughly 7 events.
- maybe.quoteme is dense, but every cue there is a recognisable needle drop doing a joke. Utility clicks dilute that.

**Concessions**
- B's "cue after the line, in the gap" is more precise than my "on the reveal". I adopt it.
- The caption as the second joke, but/therefore, and the scripted-hook-plus-bullets method are all better than anything I covered.
- My hook #4 copies Elliot's question format too closely. That borders on naming the reference.

**Three changes to B's beats**
1. **0–3s:** open with "i'm not a developer. i built my website by giving it editing notes." Hard cut to the site's intro with its own impact hit.
2. **Cues:** drop the per-note clicks. Keep about 3 joke cues (scratch on the lag, crickets on the dots, one more) and leave the take dry.
3. **The turn:** make it causal ("the lag made me cut things, and cutting is what made it mine"). Let him find the take on camera; no written aphorism.
