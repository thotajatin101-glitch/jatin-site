# Agent B: scripts and retention (deadpan journey / essay reel tone)

## Sourced
- **Elliot's comedy is deliberate, built from improv and acting.** He calls his page "honest depictions of the creative experience". He found that playful, honest posts beat polished ones, and he is open about mistakes, rates and process. https://utsvertigo.com.au/discover/in-conversation-with-elliotisacoolguy . He comes from theatre and uses comedy to make his design shareable: https://www.everand.com/podcast/594526181/Elliotisacoolguy-Ep-06 . I found **no public breakdown of how he scripts or edits**, so treat anything here about his method as observed, not sourced.
- **Causation, not "and then".** Beats should link with "but" (conflict) or "therefore" (progress). That rule comes from Parker and Stone's NYU class. https://perell.com/note/but-therefore-rule/ ; Tony Zhou applies it to film: https://nofilmschool.com/2015/04/tony-zhou-storytelling-video-essay-f-fake-therefore-but-and-then
- **Script the hook word for word and bullet the rest.** Reading a full script sounds like performing. Bullets need specifics, because a vague bullet ("talk about lag") leaves you blank on camera. https://captions.ai/blog/how-to-write-short-form-video-scripts , https://www.speakflow.com/blog/posts/full-script-or-talking-points-teleprompter , https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-questions/do-your-videos-need-a-script-or-will-it-kill-your-natural-delivery
- **Comic timing in the cut.** Hold a beat of awkward pause before the cut. Drop the music out so a line lands. Cut to the reaction fast. Bring the next clip's audio in early (an L-cut shortened for short-form). https://air.io/youtube-hacks/editing-tricks-for-fast-paced-funny-videos , https://tokcount.com/blog/14-micro-cut-transitions-that-keep-completion-rates-above-60-percent
- **Use sound effects sparingly.** Used well, they reset attention and mark transitions. Too many backfire. https://fish.audio/blog/using-sound-effects-for-short-form-content , https://joinbrands.com/blog/joinbrands-creator-masterclass-live-4-editing-for-virality/
- **Endings.** A long "thanks for watching" outro gives viewers permission to leave. Ending on the payoff or looping back to the opening frame helps rewatches. The rewatch evidence is anecdotal, and Instagram does not publish how it weights replays. https://async.com/blog/reel-retention-tips/ , https://www.influencers-time.com/loop-optimized-reels-the-seamless-cut-that-boosts-rewatch-ra/
- **The self-check** "did this feel like me, what felt forced" counts as an authenticity test. https://vidiq.com/blog/post/how-to-create-youtube-video-essay/

## Observed (from the references as described in the brief)
- **The TouchDesigner video uses the narrator's mock-epic frame** ("this sick man… this is his story"). The grand frame plus a small, petty struggle is the joke, and he never winks at it.
- **The maybe.quoteme reel puts its sound cue right after a sincere line,** never on top of it. Each cue is a well-known needle drop, and its job is to undercut the speaker, never to laugh at the audience. The take arrives softly ("maybe we should all…"): a suggestion, not a lesson.
- **The caption carries a second voice:** lowercase, self-deprecating, adding a joke the video didn't tell.
- **A podcast-clip look** (two mics, mid-thought cut-in) reads as overheard, so the viewer's guard drops.

## Opinion (mine)
- **What reads as try-hard:**
  - an announced hook ("you won't believe…");
  - a sound effect on every line;
  - stock "bro" energy;
  - zooms on every sentence;
  - a lesson stated as a rule;
  - a "follow for part 2" ask;
  - explaining the joke.
- **Deadpan only works if the face stays still and the edit does the laughing.** He is an editor, so this suits him: the cut is the punchline.
- **The struggles are the content.** The lag on friends' laptops and the dots in Safari are concrete, visual and a little humiliating. That's exactly what makes the self-deprecation honest, not performed.
- **Cut to the real thing on screen.** Show the dots, the stutter, an actual note he typed. Proof replaces any need to explain.
- **No reference names:** describe the feeling ("I wanted it to feel like a game menu"). Viewers fill the gap themselves.

## Script rules (for Jatin)
1. Write the first line word for word. Everything after it is 4–5 specific bullets ("friend: 'why are there dots'").
2. Every beat links to the last with a **but** or a **therefore**, never "and then".
3. Say it straight and let the cut be the joke. No mugging to camera.
4. Use one sound cue per struggle beat, placed right after the line in the gap, and use no more than 3–4 across the whole reel.
5. Show the failure on screen (a screen recording or a phone photo) instead of describing it.
6. The take is one soft sentence in his own words, said as "maybe", never "you should".
7. Cut the "so basically" lead-in. Start mid-thought, as if he's halfway through telling a friend.
8. Leave the caption the second joke, in lowercase.
9. No outro. The last frame is the site working, ideally the same frame he opened on so the reel loops.

## Beat structure: website reel (~38s)
| Time | Beat | Sound / humour |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3s | Cold open, mid-thought: "so I built my own website. I'm not a developer." The site's intro plays behind him or in a cutaway. | No music yet, dead air. The flat delivery is the hook. |
| 3–9s | **Therefore:** "I built it like I edit: just giving notes. 'too slow.' 'hits too soft.'" | Quick jump cuts on each note; one small click or key sound per note. |
| 9–15s | **But:** "it lagged on my friends' laptops." | Screen recording of the stutter; music cuts out or a record scratch lands right after the line. Hold 1s of his face. |
| 15–21s | **But:** "a friend opened it in Safari and saw… dots. Just dots." | Cut to the dots screenshot, then crickets or a single tone. Deadpan hold is the punchline. |
| 21–30s | **Therefore (the turn):** "it only started feeling like mine when I cut stuff out." | Show one or two things disappearing in quick removals. Music returns, warmer. |
| 30–36s | **Take:** "maybe making something yours is mostly deciding what it isn't." | No sound effect, keep it sincere. |
| 36–38s | End on the site working, matching the opening frame, with no ask. | Music resolves on the loop seam. |

Note: the 30–36s line is a placeholder. The take has to come out in his words when he says it, or it will read as scripted.

## Critique of A

**Where we disagree:**
- **The opening sound.** I opened on "no music, dead air". A wants a sound event on the first frame, then silence under the line. **A is right.** Mosseri says every reel should have audio, and Skip Rate measures exactly those first 3s. My dead air also reads as a broken upload. The fix: the site's own impact hit on frame 1, then room tone under his line. That keeps my deadpan and A's hook. I also missed on-screen text for people watching with the sound off. I concede both.
- **Cue density.** A says one cue per beat; I said 3–4 for the whole reel. The difference is small, but A's hook #5 (a punch on every deletion) breaks both rules. Repeated hits are exactly the try-hard pattern.

**Weak spots in A:**
- **The maybe.quoteme "observed" claims** (scratch for a turn, captions saying "lol") come from the brief, not from watching the reel. A admits this but still files them as observed. My observed section has the same flaw, so neither of us has actually watched her edit.
- **The nowbam "honest clickbait" source** is a marketing blog, and I couldn't verify the stills.com "finding the joke" claim. They're plausible, but weak.
- **Hook #4 is try-hard in disguise.** It copies Elliot's title formula almost word for word. That's naming the reference through its shape, and a parenthetical works in a YouTube title but nobody says one out loud.

**Does each hook lead into the journey reel?**
1. **Yes.** It is my beats 1–2 and sets up the whole arc. Risk: "giving it notes" invites the question "notes to whom?". That's a fine curiosity gap, but if the answer is an AI tool the reel starts to promise a tool video, so he should decide whether to say it.
2. **It spends the best punchline at 0s** and turns the reel into a bug story. The dots work harder mid-reel.
3. **It leads in well.** It's a natural line, it sets up the struggle, and the judder cut proves it.
4. **It promises a tutorial or a comparison,** not his story.
5. **It gives away the ending.** It's the take, and the reel needs it to land last.
