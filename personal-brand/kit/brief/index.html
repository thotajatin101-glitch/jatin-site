01

THE IDEA

what this look is about, in one page

The site wasn't designed to be a brand, but it became one. It's built on three ideas. Hold any new piece up against them: if one is missing, it doesn't look like me yet. The point of all of it is breaking my limits, and the chain is how that shows on screen.

LOUD

Six clashing colours at full saturation on pure black. They clash on purpose. Black isn't empty space: it's the big wedges between the colours.

PRINTED

It should look screen-printed, not rendered: ink dots, ordered dither, hard black ink rules, and a static red/cyan misregistration. The texture lives in the image, never as a layer over it.

UNCHAINED

Tension, then release. Things arrive held: chained, floating, waiting. Then a yank, one white frame, and they break free. The visitor or viewer is usually the one who sets off the break.

WHERE IT COMES FROM

  • My logo animation: the whole look. Shards radiating from the centre, thick black strokes, the soft black hold behind the name.
  • Persona 5 menus: the layout. Slanted plates, one huge accent wedge, a word slammed across it, every input answered with a hit.
  • Pop-art explosion title cards: the impact. Frames stepped on 2s, halftone, starbursts, chromatic aberration.
02

COLOUR

six inks, black, white, chrome

One accent leads each piece, the way each menu item owns its own colour. The other colours turn up only as shards, sparks and the red/cyan fringe. Black takes up most of the frame, and never grey it out.

HOW MUCH OF EACH

black ~50% · lead accent ~30% · support colours ~12% · white type ~8%

EVERY GRADIENT IS A DITHERED RAMP

Gradients don't blend smoothly. They step through a 4×4 Bayer dither between five tones: deep shade → accent → near-white. These are the ramps the site uses:

acid → green (logo shards)
cyan → deep (logo shards)
pink accent ramp
chrome (the SKILLS card)

In Resolve or Photoshop, build the ramp, then use a Bayer/ordered dither (not diffusion) at 3px cells, 5px on huge areas.

TYPE

one loud face, one quiet one, two specialists

Cyberway Riders does the shouting and Plex Mono does the talking. Display type is never level: tilt it 2–6° and give it the glitch split.

CUT · SHOOT
Display · Cyberway Riders

The wordmark and every heading, title card and big word. Always ALL CAPS, tilted, glitch-split. The master file is CyberwayRiders-lg97d.ttf in the site folder.

Body · IBM Plex Mono

Captions, descriptions, credits, small labels. Labels are uppercase and tracked wide (.2em+). Callout plates use italic 700, ink on silver. Never put the glitch split on mono: it doubles the letters.

SPACING IS THE RULE THAT SAVES IT

Cyberway has long spurs. Packed tight, the letters fuse into one blob. Letter-spacing is .14em (in Resolve, tracking ≈ 1.14) and word spacing .28em. Never go tighter.

04

TEXTURE

print, not filter

The texture should feel naturally in the image, like ink on paper. If you can see it as a pattern sitting on top, it's wrong.

yes · ink dots

Small ink dots on the colour: 5px pitch, rotated 23°, about 62% black. They disappear into the black shards on their own.

no · big grid

Dots at 14px, squared up. Reads as "some pattern".

no · holes in black

Holes punched in a black layer. Reads as a layer on top of everything.

THE GLITCH SPLIT · STATIC, THREE SIZES

JATINSM · 2px · small titles
JATINMD · 4px · headings
JATINLG · 8px · the hero word

Cyan offset right, pink offset left, a hard black drop down-right. Sizes are for 1080px-wide frames, so scale them with the frame. On type sitting on a flooded accent, drop to one light offset. Below about 30px tall, keep the split to 1px.

ALSO

  • Film grain: light and static. The only overlay allowed.
  • Chromatic burst: red/cyan rip about 13px apart and snap back in 0.4s. Fire it on a cut or a hit, never on idle.
  • Ink rule: thick black outline on shapes and stickers, plus a hard drop shadow. No soft shadows, no glow.
no · scanlines

They moiré against the dots and read as noise. Cut entirely.

no · vignette

The frame stays bright to the edges. Darkness only behind the subject.

yes · the hold
JATIN

One soft black blob hugging the title. It's the only darkness that's allowed to feather.

SHAPES

the kit of parts

Everything radiates from one vanishing point: shards, streaks, the grid floor, the chain. Pick the point first, then build the frame around it.

SHARDS

Radiate from the point, full bleed to the edges, thick black strokes. Big black wedges between the colour. Some get a stripe or hatch fill, never the black ones.

EDITS

THE WEDGE

A huge triangle pointing left, in the lead accent, dotted, with one word slammed across it. It's the signature frame.

PLATES

Near-black slanted bars bleeding off the left edge, stepping down like stairs. The selected plate floods with the accent and grows from its left edge.

DEPTH

A bloom at the point, a glowing perspective floor, light streaks with white-hot cores. This is what gives the flat print its space.

STICKERS

P5 stars, four-point sparkles, a bolt, and the pixel chain link (stepped ring, gradient face, ink rule, red/cyan fringe). Sprinkle them, don't wallpaper with them.

Hey, I'm Jatin. I never stop experimenting.

PLATES OF COPY

Silver dithered plate, ink rule, ink drop, italic mono. Highlight one phrase with an accent marker strip, ink on colour.

PHOTOS IN THE LOOK

  • Cut people out in black and white and let the colour come from the world around them. A thick accent rim plus red/cyan ghosts make it a sticker.
  • His work itself (the photos and edits) stays clean: no dots, no scanlines on the work. The language is the frame around it.

MOTION

timing is the deliverable · numbers at 24fps

Every piece follows one beat: hold → yank → snap → slam → settle. The set-up is slow and smooth so the hit can be a single frame. The contrast is the impact.

HOLD / FLOATSTRAINSLAM · ON 2sHITS EVERY 8fOUT

MoveTimingHow it should feel
Float (the set-up)as long as it takesSlow zero-g drift, gentle curves. Smooth every frame. Not flowy: barely moving, so the pull lands.
Yank1 frameSlack gone instantly, then a damped thud (camera jolt, scale pop) gone in ~4 frames.
Snap / impact1 white frameFull white flash, shockwave, speed lines, debris flung toward the lens. Debris clears by ~14 frames.
Slam & chaoson 2sShapes re-roll every 2 frames and hold across both. That stepped timing is the hand-drawn look. Don't smooth it into tweens.
Beat hitsevery 8 frames180bpm. Scale punch + split spike + a slice glitch on each hit.
Transition~12 framesNeon outlines whip in (5f) → white flash (1f) → fills slam oversized 1.14 and snap to rest (1f) → cut under it → fills drain off downstream (7f). They leave the way they came.
Selection / cut accent10 framesChromatic burst: ~13px rip collapsing to zero. One per change.
Object entrance~24 framesOne smooth ease-out move (cubic/quart) with a single turn. No stop-start.
Title re-slam~6 framesSmear → overshoot → settle.
IdlenoneNothing twitches on its own. Only a slow flow or turn in the world. Motion answers input or the beat.
  • Colour changes are hard cuts (steps), never fades.
  • Opening S-curves run smooth at full frame rate. Stepped timing starts at the snap, and only then.
  • Hold the frame. If a note says "too slow" it's usually the set-up; "hits too soft" means the hit isn't one frame.

NEVER

every one of these was tried and cut

Taste by subtraction. These came out of the site one note at a time. Don't bring them back in a post or an edit.

IDLE FLICKERlooks like a problem with a TVNo twitching, no grain jumps, no idle glitch. Glitch fires on a hit or an input only.
SCANLINESMoiré against the dots, reads as noise. The work stays clean.
VIGNETTEEdges stay bright like the logo video. The hold blob is the only dark.
GRUNGENo distressed fonts or eroded edges. Texture comes from print, not damage.
A VISIBLE GRIDsome patternBig squared-up dots. Keep them small and angled.
A PLAIN SLIDEjust a slideA wipe isn't a transition. Trace, flash, slam, drain.
RANDOM FILLERempty and random, no intentionEvery opening sets something up. If it isn't tension, cut it.
FLOWY SET-UPStoo flowyThe float is slow and barely there, so the pull can hit.
FLAT 3Dit feels flatObjects get real depth, lit hard, with the dots printed on the model, not over it.
AN OVERLAYlooks like an overlay rather than a textureTexture lives in the surface.
CURSOR / CAMERA DRIFTThe world holds still. Only the subject reacts.
CHEESY LINEStoo cheesyNo slogans. Say it plainly.
