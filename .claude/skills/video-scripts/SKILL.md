# Hooks and scripts for his videos

He is on camera, talking. These rules came from his notes across rounds 1–6 of the identity reel and from two
research agents that cross-checked each other (2026-11-21). The research and sources are in
`personal-brand/videos/HOOKS-AND-SCRIPTS.md` and `personal-brand/videos/research/`. Follow this file. If he corrects
something, update this file in the same turn.

## The tone
**Sincere, said straight. The edit makes the joke.** He never acts funny; the cut and the sound do it. His tone
references: elliotisacoolguy (deadpan, lowercase first-person titles that admit a limit), digital_spaghetti, and
maybe.quoteme (a sincere personal take with sound cues as punchlines).

- **Conversational, never trying too hard** (his words: "more of conversation than a script", "natural tone rather
  then trying to hard").
- **Talk like a friend:** contractions, uneven sentence lengths, one idea per sentence, one admission of doubt.
- **Words:** taste, style, identity. Not "branding" (to him it "takes the meaning out of stuff"). "Brand" is only for a
  description or tags.
- **Don't name his references** on camera (games, shows) unless he asks. Describe the feeling, or show it.
- **Never write his experiences for him.** Use only facts from the repo's logs or his messages. Anything only he
  knows goes in a **[YOU: …]** marker.

## The hook
1. Name the problem or the angle in the first 1.5s; the visual proves it by 3s (Instagram counts skips in the first 3s).
2. One sentence, first person, said flat. Repeat it as lowercase on-screen text so it works muted.
3. Lead with a confession or a limit, not a credential.
4. Promise only what the video delivers. No claim he'd have to walk back (he rejected "I never designed this").
5. Don't spend the best punchline in the hook; save it for the middle.
6. Never: "you need to", "stop scrolling", "nobody talks about", "here's the part nobody tells you", emoji-caps text.
7. Sound: a hit on frame 1 (ideally from his own work), then quiet room tone under the line. Never dead air at frame 0.

## The script
1. **The hook is the only line written word for word.** Everything else is 4–5 *specific* talking points (vague
   bullets leave him blank on camera). Give an example line for each point, marked as a guide, not a script.
2. **Each beat causes the next** ("but" / "so"), never "and then".
3. **Show every struggle** as a screen recording or a photo. Don't just describe it.
4. **Sound:** about 3 joke cues in the whole reel, each a recognisable sound landing in the gap *after* a line, never on
   top of it. No clicks on every cut, no hit on every word. The take stays dry.
5. **On camera stays calm.** His site's glitch edit language (24fps, on 2s, red/cyan split) lives on the B-roll only.
6. **The take:** one soft sentence he finds on camera. Never "you should", and never an aphorism written to be quoted.
7. **No outro, no CTA, no "follow for part 2".** End on the payoff, ideally matching the first frame so it loops.
8. **Caption:** lowercase, one or two lines, telling a second joke the video didn't. No hashtag wall, no comment bait.

Banned phrasing: three-part escalations, more than one "not X but Y", one-word punch lists ("Taste. Is. Everything."),
slogans as the lesson, a recap at the end.

## What to deliver
Put it in `personal-brand/videos/YYYY-MM-DD-name/reel.md` (or `script.md` for long form), in this shape:
1. **His notes, in his words, per round**, plus a line on what changed and why.
2. **Tone line + how to shoot it:** say the hook word for word; talk the rest as if a friend asked; do 2–3 loose
   takes, and the edit finds the length.
3. **A table: Time | You (talking points) | On screen | Sound.**
4. **Caption.**
5. **Your calls:** each [YOU] fact to confirm, with my pick where there's a choice.

Length: Insta reels are 30–45s of finished edit. Talk longer on the day.

Answer him short and direct, in edit terms. Give one recommended hook with a short defence, not five options.
Commit the file by name (never `git add -A`).
