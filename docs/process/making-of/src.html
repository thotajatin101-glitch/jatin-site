00

The brief

August 2026 · two references, merged on purpose

The concept was never "a portfolio site". It was a deliberate merge of two things Jatin already loved.

Reference 1 · his own JATIN logo animation

His intro logo, cut in DaVinci Resolve and Fusion, supplies the entire visual language: loud saturated colour on pure black, shards radiating from screen centre with thick black strokes, a fine halftone screen, a chromatic glitch split on the type, and a soft dark blob that hugs the wordmark.

Reference 2 · a Persona 5 Royal game menu

Supplies the interaction and layout. One landing screen. Items you select. Sections that slam open over it. Hard cuts, not fades. Type that is always tilted, never level.

The rule that came from merging them

Not a scrolling website. A title sequence, then a menu. Everything should feel like it came out of the logo video, and behave like a game.

The palette, straight from the video

ACID #e8ff00
CYAN #00f0d8
MAGENTA #c81ee0
PURPLE #7a12b0
GREEN #39e639
PINK #ff2e88
BLACK, and plenty of it

Loud and clashing on purpose. The black shards between the colour are part of the look, not empty space. The typeface is his own, Cyberway Riders, used for the wordmark and every heading (including this document).

01

The first draft

24 October 2026 · the foundation

His framing of it: the first draft is the foundation everything after was built on top of. Not a throwaway. The palette, the shard field, the menu model, the big accent wedge and the portrait all start here, and every chapter after this reworks one of these pieces.

  • Intro: a beat-for-beat recreation of his logo video. Shard field strobing, wordmark centre.
  • Menu: five plates (ABOUT, PROJECTS, SKILLS, EXPERIENCE, CONTACT) beside a giant accent wedge with the selected word slammed across it.
  • Sections: tilted headings over two clashing colour bands, with cards.
  • ABOUT: his photo, background removed, pushed to high-contrast black and white.

What changed later: the intro was rebuilt from new references, EXPERIENCE was removed, the clashing two-band sections became one accent wedge per screen, and ABOUT became a poster.

02

Getting the look right

October 2026 · mostly by cutting things

The texture of the site was found by taking things away. Each line below is something that was built, looked at, and cut on his note. Together they are the reason the site looks printed rather than filtered.

What was triedHis noteWhy it went, and what replaced it
Vignetteremoved it, explicitlyHis video stays bright to the edges. The frame never darkens. This became a permanent rule: no vignette, ever.
Grunge, distressed typecutTexture should come from the glitch shadow, not from eroded letters.
Animated glitch, grain jumps"looks like a problem with a TV"Idle motion read as a broken screen. The glitch split is now static and only moves on input.
Scanlinescut entirelyFine lines moiréd against the halftone dots and read as noise.
Halftone as holes punched in blackrejectedRead as a layer on top of everything, even over the black shards. Now ink dots printed on the colour, which vanish into the blacks like real screenprint.
Big 14px square dots"some pattern"Now 5px dots turned 23°. A fine print screen that never resolves into a visible grid.
Tight letter spacingPROJECTS "reading as one blob"Cyberway Riders has long swashes that fuse. Tested side by side at three settings; one setting won and is now used on every heading.

What stayed: a static chromatic split on display type (cyan left, pink right, hard black drop), headings always tilted a few degrees, light static film grain, and the halftone as a fine print screen.

03

The menu becomes Persona 5

October 2026 · from his sketch
a Persona-5-Royal system menu, with my palette as the twist
  • Staircase plates, from his sketch. Each plate bleeds off the left edge, each one down is shorter, and the right ends are cut on one continuous diagonal down the stack.
  • The selected plate floods with its accent colour on a hard cut, type flips to black, and it pushes out to the right. No fades anywhere.
  • A chromatic burst on every selection. The red and cyan channels rip apart and snap back in under half a second. It is the only glitch that moves, and only on input.
  • The giant wedge is the signature. A huge triangle in the selected colour fills the right of the screen, with the item's name slammed across it.
  • The wedge heading is measured, not guessed. He asked twice for it to fill the space; the first attempts left half the wedge empty on wide screens.
EXPERIENCE removed.

Four items, four colours, carried through every screen after: ABOUT cyan, PROJECTS pink, SKILLS green, CONTACT acid.

The wedge comes alive · three rounds

  1. R1
    not too harshA flowing gradient inside the triangle, kept gentle.
  2. R2
    too subtle, more motion and contrast, keep the smooth blendWider colour range, faster flow.
  3. R3
    bands too narrow, make them flowy and wider, add a small ditherWide swaying bands and a pixel dither between colour steps: print texture, never a smear.
  4. +
    give it some personalityThe same moving dither now runs inside every solid shape on the site: plates, bands, cards, buttons.
04

Depth

October 2026 · from Spider-Verse references
more depth, intentionality and personality

His references were Spider-Verse style frames. What makes those read as a space rather than a flat poster was broken down and rebuilt:

  • One vanishing point everything radiates from or recedes into.
  • A glowing perspective grid floor and ceiling flowing toward the viewer.
  • Light streaks with white-hot cores, and dark ink streaks cutting the bright field.
  • Layers that move at different depths with the pointer (parallax).
  • On every selection the whole world eases to that item's colour, with a burst of streaks.

What was kept out

Motion stays to four coherent moves: flow, turn, parallax, burst on input. No twinkle, no flicker, nothing that moves on its own for the sake of it. The sky only brightens toward the point; edges are never darkened.

The shards changed with it

Fewer and narrower, starting further out, dissolving as they recede into the light source, and turning slowly around it. The gaps between them now show the sky.

05

The intro, rebuilt

October 2026 · eleven rounds on the opening alone

New references from him: two pop-art explosion title intros. The brief: explosive composition, electric colour, comic print textures (halftone, stripes, ASCII dithering), chromatic aberration, glitching, shapes expanding and shattering frame by frame, motion aggressive and rhythmic.

The cadence is the look. It runs on a 24fps clock, and every shape re-rolls on twos, so each drawing holds for two frames. That stepped timing is what makes it hit like hand-drawn impact frames. It is never smoothed into tweens.

The opening took three tries

  1. 1
    empty and random, no intentionRandom sparks on a HUD. Cut.
  2. 2
    after the break I don't want the name to disappear, continue with the chaosA neon outline build left a black screen with lines. Cut.
  3. 3
    JATIN chained, then the aggressive break unleashes the animationThe chained lock-up. The tension is the intention; the snap is what releases everything after it.

Then it went 3D

  1. 3D
    I want the chains and name logo to be 3DJATIN became a solid slab; the chains became real links that pass in front of and behind it.
  2. keep
    I do like logo being chained up, don't remove thatThe two X straps stay. Shape first like the 2D chain, then texture: dithering instead of halftone, colours untouched.
  3. hug
    the chains are bulging, it should be closer to the logo as it is stuck to itStraps pulled tight to the slab.
  4. float
    the chains float like zero gravity, then suddenly and quickly tighten upSlack drifting curves, then a one-frame yank.
  5. weight
    a bit too flowy … the pull needs more weight … a small shake to add impactSlower float, harder yank, a damped thud through camera, word and chains.
  6. horns
    why is there extra chain spiking up around the logo?Strap ends poked past the J and N. Flattened to stay inside the word.

Transitions

October 2026 · half a second, symmetric
  1. 1
    just a slideA sliding colour-band wipe. Cut.
  2. 2
    matching outlines, more impact, shorterA shatter version. Cut in favour of the next one.
  3. 3
    The intro's opening in miniature. Neon outlines whip in, one white impact frame, the fills slam, and everything retracts the way it came. The screen swaps underneath at 240ms, fully covered.

Every screen change on the site uses it. When the intro hands over to the menu, the logo moves to the corner behind the slam as an instant cut, never a visible slide.

07

Chains

October 2026 · from his red-line sketches

The chains are made from the site's own pixel "puzzle pieces", not real chain links: stepped pixel rings and bars, with the two gradients from his logo video (acid to green, cyan to deep).

The menu chain · four rounds

  1. 1
    slowly come out transparencyA slow spiral fading links in and out. Rejected.
  2. 2
    A flat ring around the wedge.
  3. 3
    crookedTraced from his first sketch. Wobbly, and it started on the wrong edge.
  4. 4
    Now: one smooth curve through six points of his second sketch, from behind the triangle, looping behind the plates and sweeping toward the viewer, growing from far to near the whole way. Nothing fades.

Texture

the halftone on the chains looks too much like overlay rather than a texture, and it's a bit too big

The dots were halved, printed into each link's face only, and made tonal: they swell in the shade and disappear in the highlight.

Where they appear

The menu chain recolours with every selection and follows into PROJECTS, SKILLS and CONTACT. The intro is held in them. The computer and the skill card both arrive chained up and break free.

About becomes a poster

26 – 27 October 2026 · “Have you seen this DIRECTOR?”

ABOUT went through two complete designs. First a Persona 5 collage modelled on a "KREFTON" poster he sent. Then he brought a new reference and a new night photo.

I want the about page designed like this but obviously keeping my design language. Particularly observe the text placement, how it is scaled and the perspective changes. And replace my image with the one I uploaded.

The reference was a "Have you seen this MAN?" poster. Its banners are wedges, so the headline grows as it travels across them. That became HAVE YOU SEEN THIS DIRECTOR? on top and his own line turned into the punchline at the bottom: if it doesn't cut through the noise… IT DOESN'T SHIP.

The rounds

  1. R2
    my photo feels too small with all the big bars … just remove all the text so we can figure out the alignmentText out, bars thinner, portrait bigger.
  2. R3
    you are leaving a lot of empty space in the boxesFrom his marked-up screenshot: photo above the top bar, below the bottom one.
  3. R4
    the crop looks very unnatural … the outline looks like a sketch pen, make it fade outwards using cyan and hot pinkNew hair matte; a neon halo replaced the stamped outline.
  4. R5
    keep 2D chains along the path with infinite motionStraight chains along the banner edges, scrolling forever.
  5. glow
    a dither animation going outwards for that glowThe halo became a pixel dither flowing away from the figure, soft, behind the bubble.

Projects: the computer

October 2026 · a retro monitor, built without a 3D library

He gave a rendered reference: a soft beige vintage monitor with a deep bezel on a flat desktop base with two knobs. It is drawn by one shader so the site stays one file.

Shaping it · his notes in order

  1. 1
    edges too rounded
  2. 2
    sides should align with the base
  3. 3
    knobs are pointy, they should be flat-topped cylinders, like buttons
  4. 4
    too plainVents, a green power LED, two floppy drives.
  5. 5
    a little curved, not too heavyThe glass bulges just enough.
  6. 6
    A cinematic lighting reference replaced the print look: cool key light, deep cool shadows, bright rims on every bevel. FLUX and BTN stickers on the side, which he kept.

Then it came alive

  • It arrives chained up and breaks out. His note: more aggressive, with motion and a flash → white flash, shockwave, speed lines, links flung at the camera, a shake.
  • The screen boots in the site's language and lands on a big pink CLICK ME!!. Click, and the camera dollies into the glass.
  • Inside is JATIN OS: a small operating system with his folders. Folders browse with smooth eases; stepped motion here read to him as lag.
  • He reported the loading as laggy, so the heavy parts now only work where the computer actually is on screen.

12

What he was specific about

The rules that came out of the notes

Read across all the rounds, his notes keep landing on the same few principles. These are the ones that now govern every new piece.

Motion only on intent

Nothing twitches on its own. Idle glitch read as "a problem with a TV". Glitch fires on input, once, then settles.

Stepped where it hits, smooth where it travels

The intro's explosion holds each drawing for two frames, like hand-drawn impact. But the float, the folders and the opening are smooth; stepped motion there "reads as lag".

Print, not filter

Texture is a screenprint: small angled ink dots and pixel dither on the colour. No grunge, no scanlines over the work, no overlays that sit on top.

Never darken the frame

No vignette, ever. The only darkness is the soft blob behind the name and the black shards themselves.

Weight and impact

"No weight to it", "the pull needs more weight", "more aggressive, with a flash". Things should land: a yank, a thud, a shake, then stillness.

Intention over decoration

"Empty and random, no intention" killed a whole intro. Every element has to set something up or pay something off.

Fill the space

"Leaving a lot of empty space", "fill the wedge". He asked twice for the wedge heading to fill its room; the poster bars were reshaped to his lines.

Material has to be believable

Chrome that looks like plastic, a dusty foil, edges "too liquidy and blobby": the card took 27 rounds because surfaces had to feel real.

The work comes first

Fullscreen photos, zoom and drag, scanlines off his photos, one BACK button. When it is his work on screen, the style steps aside.

Simple beats clever

"Remove exit altogether … that's better and simple." One way out, labelled with where it goes.

It's all one world

Each section wears its menu colour, carries the same wedge and chain, and buttons inside the OS borrow the menu plates. Continuity across every screen.

Keep what he likes

"I do like the logo being chained up, don't remove that." "Keep the corner edge part only." Changes are surgical; the parts he liked stay put.
